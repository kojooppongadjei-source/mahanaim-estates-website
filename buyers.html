Know your numbers

What you'll actually need to bring.

The two numbers buyers ask about most: earnest money when you make an offer, and the down payment at closing.

The rate

Typically 1–3% of the purchase price; it can run higher in competitive markets like Lancaster or Baltimore.

Where it goes

Held in escrow, then applied toward your down payment at closing.

Your protection

With a mortgage contingency, it's usually returned if you can't secure the loan.

At every showing

Four questions worth asking.

Why is the seller moving?

Their motivation and timeline can shape your negotiation strategy.

How old are the major systems?

Roof, HVAC, plumbing and electrical tell you what maintenance is coming.

Any known issues or past repairs?

Ask about water damage, foundation or pests, and whether repairs were professional.

What do utilities usually run?

Typical heating, cooling and water bills help you build a real monthly budget.

Good to know

Buyer questions, answered plainly.

Do I need a down payment right now?

Not to start. A lender can walk you through what's realistic for your situation before you owe anyone a dollar.

How fast can we move?

As fast as you need to. Some buyers tour their first home within days of that first conversation.

What does it cost to work with you?

In most transactions, the seller covers the buyer's agent commission — we'll go over the specifics together up front.

How do I know if I'm truly ready to buy?

Financially: steady income, a reliable record of paying bills, and manageable long-term debt. Emotionally: you need more space or want the freedom to make a place your own.

What is a pre-approval, and why does it matter?

It's a letter from a lender stating how much they're willing to lend you. It keeps your search inside your budget and shows sellers you're a serious, qualified buyer.

Can I back out if the inspection finds problems?

Yes, if your contract has an inspection contingency. You can negotiate repairs, ask for a credit, or cancel without penalty if you can't agree.

How long does closing take?

Typically 30 to 45 days after your offer is accepted, which covers inspection, appraisal and the lender's paperwork.

Plain-English glossary

The words you'll hear on the way to closing.

Pre-approval

A lender's letter stating how much they'll lend you; it makes your offer stronger.

Earnest money (EMD)

Good-faith money submitted with your offer, typically 1–3% of the price, held in escrow and applied to your down payment at closing.

Escrow

A neutral third party that holds funds, like your earnest money, until the contract terms are met.

Appraisal

An unbiased professional opinion of value, required by your lender.

Inspection

A detailed physical check of the property for repairs or safety issues.

Contingency

A condition in the contract, like inspection or financing, that must be met for the sale to proceed.

Title insurance

Protection against future claims or disputes over who owns the property.

Equity

The difference between what your home is worth and what you still owe on it.
