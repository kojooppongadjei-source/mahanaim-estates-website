How to Sell a House in Pennsylvania: A Step-by-Step Guide

By Eunice Osei-Manu · 8 min read

Selling a house in Pennsylvania follows a fairly predictable sequence, but each step has real decisions attached to it. Here's the full process, so nothing catches you off guard along the way.

Step 1: Price it against real comps

Pricing starts with what's actually sold nearby in the last three to six months, adjusted for condition, square footage, and upgrades. Pricing too high and chasing it down with cuts almost always nets less than pricing accurately from day one.

Step 2: Handle the disclosures

Pennsylvania requires sellers to complete a Seller's Property Disclosure Statement, covering known issues with the property. Getting this right protects you from disputes after closing, and being upfront about a home's condition rarely scares off a serious buyer the way people expect.

Step 3: Prep the home before it's listed

A pre-listing walkthrough can catch issues, an aging roof, an outdated electrical panel, and similar items, before a buyer's inspector finds them first. Sometimes a modest repair pays for itself several times over in the final sale price.

Step 4: List and market it properly

Professional photos, a clear listing description, and getting the home in front of active buyers matters more than most sellers expect. A listing that looks rushed tends to sit longer, even at a fair price.

Step 5: Review offers and negotiate

Offers aren't just about price. Contingencies, timelines, and requested repairs all factor into which offer is actually strongest. In a multiple-offer situation, the highest number isn't always the best one to accept.

Worth knowing: A cash offer with a fast, clean close is sometimes worth more to a seller than a slightly higher offer that comes with financing contingencies and a longer timeline.

Step 6: Get through inspection and appraisal

Once under contract, the buyer's inspection findings often lead to a second round of negotiation, repairs, credits, or a price adjustment. If the buyer is financing, the home also needs to appraise at or above the agreed price.

Step 7: Closing day

At closing, ownership transfers, funds are disbursed, and Pennsylvania's transfer tax gets paid, typically split between buyer and seller depending on what was negotiated. From here, the sale is final.

The bottom line

Selling in Pennsylvania is a sequence of decisions more than a single event: how to price it, what to disclose, how to prep it, and how to evaluate offers when they come in. Having someone walk each of these with you turns a stressful process into a manageable one.
