Before the sign goes in

Three things that make buyers fall for it.

Declutter & depersonalize

Clearing excess items lets buyers see the square footage and picture their own family and belongings in the space.

Strategic repairs

Fresh paint, curb appeal and lighting. Small fixes now prevent big hurdles at inspection — and a fresh mat and tidy landscaping go a long way.

The deep-clean glow

A sparkling home tells buyers the property has been well maintained.

Showing etiquette

Six habits that turn showings into offers.

Be absent

Leave at least 15 minutes before a showing so buyers can talk freely and picture themselves living there.

Set the senses

No strong air fresheners or candles. TV off; soft instrumental music at most. A comfortable temperature keeps buyers lingering.

Lights on, doors open

Every lamp and overhead light on, even by day. Interior doors open (except closets and bathrooms) for flow.

Secure the essentials

Put away jewelry, small electronics, collectibles and all medications. Take pets with you or crate them somewhere quiet.

Stay flexible

The first week is the busiest. A missed showing could be a missed offer.

Daily broom-clean

Sink clear of dishes, bathroom counters wiped, beds made.

Weighing offers

Price vs. terms

The highest number isn't always the strongest offer. Closing date, inspection and appraisal contingencies, down payment and mortgage type all count.

Good to know

Seller questions, answered plainly.

What's my home actually worth?

Send us the address and we'll put together a real comparison of recent, similar sales nearby — no obligation attached.

Do I need to renovate my kitchen before listing?

Not necessarily. Often fresh neutral paint and professional staging return more than a remodel. We'll walk through and give you a prioritized list of what actually puts money in your pocket.

Do I have to leave for every showing?

Ideally, yes. We'll build a flexible showing schedule that keeps eyes on the property while respecting your family's routine.

What if the inspection finds an issue?

We handle the tough conversation with the buyer's agent and work through repair requests with you — what's reasonable to fix and what to negotiate — to keep the deal on track.

What if it doesn't appraise for the sale price?

With multiple offers we often negotiate appraisal-gap coverage up front. If an appraisal comes in low, we challenge it with data or negotiate a middle ground that protects your bottom line.

How long will it take to sell?

Days on market vary, but the goal is strong activity in the first 7 days. Once an offer is accepted, closing usually takes 30–45 days.

What are my costs as a seller?

Plan for commission, the PA/MD transfer tax (often split with the buyer) and any attorney or title fees. You'll get a Seller Net Sheet before we list, so you know what you'll walk away with.
